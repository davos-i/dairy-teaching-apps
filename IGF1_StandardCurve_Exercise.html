Background

What is a standard curve? In an ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay), we measure the concentration of a protein by comparing the absorbance of our unknown sample to a standard curve — a graph built from samples of known concentration. The curve lets us read off concentration directly from absorbance.

In this exercise you are running a sandwich ELISA for bovine IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1), a hormone important in growth and milk production. Serum IGF-1 in dairy cows typically ranges from ~30 ng/mL in early postpartum cows up to ~150 ng/mL during mid-lactation, and higher in growing heifers. Your plate reader has measured absorbance at 450 nm for a series of IGF-1 standards spanning 0–300 ng/mL. You will:

  1. Generate your personal dataset (unique to you)
  2. Calculate mean absorbance for each standard
  3. Build your standard curve in Excel
  4. Use the curve to calculate the concentration of an unknown sample
  5. Check your key numerical results

Calculate standard curve

In Excel, follow these steps:

  1. Subtract the blank: subtract the mean A₄₅₀ of the blank (0 ng/mL standard) from the mean A₄₅₀ of every other standard. The blank-corrected blank itself becomes 0.000.
  2. Plot your chart: create an XY Scatter chart with Absorbance (A₄₅₀) on the X-axis and IGF-1 Concentration (ng/mL) on the Y-axis. Do not include the blank point (it is now 0,0 and will sit on the origin).
  3. Add a linear trendline: right-click the data series → Add Trendline → Linear. Tick Set intercept to 0 and Display equation on chart.

Calculate unknown sample concentration

Using your trendline equation from Excel: your equation is in the form y = mx (where y = concentration, x = absorbance). Substitute your sample's blank-corrected absorbance for x to get the concentration directly — no rearranging needed.

Remember to blank-correct the unknown absorbance first (subtract your mean blank A₄₅₀ before plugging in).

Also plot the unknown on your Excel chart: add a second data series with just the one point so you can visually confirm it falls on the line.
